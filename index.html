ME

I write tests for a living. Every once in a while one catches something that would have been a genuinely bad day for someone, and that's the whole job.

Got started at Microsoft testing Halo and Gears of War. Testing video games is not nearly as fun as playing them, but I learned a lot, mostly about what happens when you ship software to millions of people who are specifically trying to break it.

Since then the work has covered a fair amount of ground. Web UI and cross-browser automation at Smilebox. Hardware and software integration at Precor, where the device under test was sometimes a treadmill. Retail and e-commerce systems at Rhino and lululemon. iOS automation at Fieldwire, which makes construction management software for people working on actual job sites.

Wherever I land, the questions are usually the same: what tests should actually be running, when should they run, and does anyone trust the results? The third one is almost always the hardest.

Like everyone else right now, I'm trying to figure out what AI can genuinely do in a testing workflow. Some of it is actually useful. Some of it is confident in ways that should probably worry you. I'm still not sure if it'll replace me, kill me, or neither, but I'm paying attention.

Outside of work: live theater with my family, pickleball, musical things here and there, and an unwavering loyalty to Seattle sports teams. When the Seahawks lose I'm in a bad mood. I'm working on that.
